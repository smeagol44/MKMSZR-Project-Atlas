How the ratings work

Decomp-ready means the inputs, state changes and control flow of the stated behavior are understood well enough to write a plausible C equivalent. Partial means some of that contract is mapped. Largely unknown means durable evidence is still limited.

The 600 units are editorial estimates. The heatmap shows rating totals per subsystem. Named findings document a subset of those units. A rating may apply to one bounded path within a larger subsystem.

ROM Coverage & Capacity

This view separates the supported 16 MiB clean image from the generated 32 MiB output used by global-v2. It answers four separate questions: how much has bounded ownership we understand, how much remains unclassified, how much the current MKMSZR composition owns, and how much reusable free space is actually proven. It no longer treats a coarse address grid as the main result.

Knowledge ≠ allocation. Decomp Readiness measures how well code/subsystems are understood. This page measures bounded byte ownership and capacity. Unknown bytes are not free; proof-only footprints are not production allocations.

Physical RDRAM · 4 MiB

RDRAM Coverage & Capacity

Physical bytes are counted once; KSEG0/KSEG1 are aliases, not extra memory. The useful question here is not “which 16 KiB square is colored?” but what is fixed stock ownership, what MKMSZR reserves, how much of that reservation is consumed, and what remains unknown.

Current MKMSZR reservation: exactly 16 KiB at physical 0x1AF420..0x1B3420. The first 1 KiB is Runtime V2 code/state; the remaining 15 KiB is a build-time expansion pool. Reserved-but-unused bytes are ours to plan, but are not labeled generic confirmed-free memory.
